Letters for a college freshman

Move-in day ends with a hug in a parking lot and a long drive home. The first semester brings exams, new friends, a roommate who never sleeps, and a homesick night or two they may not mention on the phone. Letters written before they go can be there for all of it.

For move-in night

  • What you thought about on the drive home
  • Something you saw them do today that made you proud
  • A small memory of their first day of kindergarten
  • Permission to call, or not call, as much as they want

To start: What did they say or do in the parking lot? Write it down before you forget it.

For the first weeks

  • Open when you’re homesick
  • Open when you don’t know anyone yet
  • Open when your roommate is driving you crazy
  • Open when you call home and nobody picks up
  • Open when you eat in the dining hall alone

To start: When were they new somewhere before? Remind them how that turned out.

For exams and grades

  • Open before your first big exam
  • Open when you get a grade you hate
  • Open when you want to change your major
  • Open when you pull an all-nighter

To start: What did you struggle with at their age? A little honesty from a parent goes a long way.

For coming home

  • Open the week before Thanksgiving
  • Open on the drive home for winter break
  • Open at the end of freshman year

To start: What will be waiting for them at home? Their room, the dog, a favorite meal.

A few things that help

Let them open some whenever they want
Freshmen rarely ask for help. Letters they can open on their own, without calling home, get used on the nights that matter.
Keep the advice light
One piece of advice per letter, at most. The rest should be about them, not about what they should do.
Write from both parents, and siblings
Different voices, different letters. A younger sibling’s letter often lands the hardest.
Start in July
August goes fast. Writing one letter a week through the summer is easier than ten the night before move-in.
