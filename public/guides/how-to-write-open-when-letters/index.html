How to write open when letters

You don’t need to be a writer. You need a list of moments, a few honest sentences for each, and a way to keep them sealed until the moment comes. This guide walks through it in the order most people find easiest.

  1. Pick the moments first

    Before writing a word, list the moments the time apart will surely bring. The first night, a bad day, a birthday, the middle, the day you meet again. Six to twelve is plenty for most goodbyes.

  2. Write the easiest one first

    Most people find “open when you miss me” or “open on the first night” easiest. Start there. Momentum matters more than order.

  3. Start with something that happened

    “Remember the night the car broke down on Lake Shore Drive?” is a better first line than “I love you so much.” A memory brings them back into the room with you.

  4. Say what you would actually say

    Read each letter out loud. If a sentence sounds like a card from a store, cross it out and say it the way you would on the phone.

  5. Keep most of them short

    Half a page is enough for most moments. Save the long one for the day you meet again.

  6. Add one thing besides words

    A photo from that day, a ticket stub, a voice note. Something they can hold or hear changes how the letter lands.

  7. Seal them, then let go

    Mark each one with its moment and trust them to open it at the right time. That trust is part of the gift.

How many open when letters should you write?

For a few weeks apart, five to eight. For a semester or a deployment, ten to twenty, plus a few that open on fixed dates so there is always one more coming. More letters are not better if they repeat each other.

Paper, or on their phone?

Paper is beautiful and easy to lose in a move. On a phone, the letters go wherever they go and can stay sealed until their day. Some people do both: a few paper letters in their bag, the rest on their phone.

What if you run out of things to say?

Ask yourself questions instead of trying to write. What did we eat the first time? What do they do when they’re nervous? What do I want them to remember about me if this gets hard? Answer one question per letter.
